**Comparison:** Five CPU candidates: constant prior, logistic regression, histogram gradient boosting (140 rounds, learning rate 0.07, 15 leaves, L2=10, internal early stopping disabled), and stable-feature variants of the latter two. Validation scores therefore contain model-development optimism. No further candidate or feature search followed the test result.

**Calibration:** A separate logistic regression maps clipped raw log odds to probabilities, `C=10`, fitted on rows 28,831–32,949. It does not refit the estimator or preprocessor. This scalar sigmoid can change the ordering if its fitted slope is negative; the recorded run's ranking is preserved. Calibration is a fixed step in this experiment, not an automatic accuracy improvement. On the subsequent policy window, raw versus calibrated Brier scores were 0.07281 and 0.07348 respectively.

There are no intersectional guarantees, multiple-comparison corrections, or identified-client independence. Do not present these tables as a fairness audit.

**Uncertainty:** Moving-block percentile intervals use 200 resamples of 100 consecutive rows. They preserve some short-range dependence but do not solve a single nonstationary time series, repeated clients, or uncertain event dates. Reported intervals do not include uncertainty from candidate selection or calibration fitting.

**Known failure modes:** Severe prior shift; changing campaign selection; unseen populations; unknown-category vectors; numeric median imputation far from current population; historical labels whose economics no longer apply; and lack of group-disjoint identity information. Neither PSI nor request validation detects all concept shift. No automatic retraining is triggered.
